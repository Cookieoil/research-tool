Vietnam's Video Game Industry: Forecasts to 2031 - An Export Factory Facing a Revenue and Regulation Test

Abstract

This paper forecasts Vietnam's video game industry to 2028-2031. It starts from the current baseline: a domestic games market of about $817 million in 2024 with 58.5 million players (86.6% mobile), inside a far larger export machine - Vietnamese studios recorded 9.6 billion downloads worldwide in 2024, making Vietnam the world's number-one mobile-game exporter, and more than 3,000 studios industrialise hyper-casual production. The defining tension is a download-versus-revenue paradox: about $0.06 revenue per daily active user excluding ads, roughly twelve times below a ~$0.70 benchmark. Into that the state dropped Decree 147/2024, which forced foreign firms to localise or exit, and set a $1 billion games-revenue target for 2030. The paper's predictions are: Vietnam keeps the export crown; downloads grow but per-user revenue stays low; domestic mobile likely misses $1bn alone; domestic publishing consolidates around licensed local partners (VNG, Garena); hyper-casual exporters stay largely outside the licence regime; VNG stays the anchor and a big overseas IPO is unlikely before 2028; Sky Mavis pivots to Ronin infrastructure with no second mass-market Web3 hit; consolidation rises as AI compresses margins; Vietnam grows as a co-development hub; and senior talent remains the binding constraint. Each prediction is stated with a horizon, a confidence value and a falsifier. These are probabilistic forecasts, not certainties.

1. Introduction

Vietnam's game industry combines world-leading export volume with an unusually thin domestic revenue base. This paper fixes a 2026 baseline and states fifteen falsifiable forecasts to 2031, each with a horizon and a watch metric, so that the sector's trajectory can be graded rather than remembered.

2. Evidence and method

Forecasting method: (1) establish a verified baseline from the most recent market and regulatory sources; (2) identify the structural drivers (export production system, low ARPU, licensing regime, capital and talent); (3) state each prediction with an explicit time horizon, a confidence value (0-1) and a falsifying observation, so claims can be tested later; (4) offer base, upside and downside scenarios. Sources include a 2026 Vietnam mobile-gaming market review and a Decree 147 analysis (Digital in Asia), Vietnam Briefing's regulatory summary, and encyclopaedic records of Sky Mavis/Axie Infinity and VNG, plus an outsourcing-studio reference. Forecast findings use engine 'forecast'; baseline facts are labelled market_data, policy_analysis, press_reporting or desk_research. No new primary data were collected.

Every claim below is a finding reproduced from the underlying research record: the claim text and its supporting detail are carried over from that record, the confidence label is the one the analysis assigned, and a bracketed number points at the reference register at the end of the paper. Analytical judgements are labelled as such, and no new data were collected for this paper.

Domestic games market was about $817M in 2024 with 58.5M players. Vietnam's domestic games market was around $817 million in 2024, with 58.5 million gamers, 86.6 percent of them on mobile - the product of cheap Android handsets, high smartphone penetration and very cheap mobile data. [1]

A second method puts 2025 mobile revenue near $825M. A separate 2025 market review cited by PocketGamer.biz estimates mobile games revenue alone at about US$825 million. Methodological differences produce a range (one tracker puts 2024 revenue near $315M), so totals should be treated as indicative. [1]

Vietnam is the world's number-one mobile game exporter by downloads. Games built by Vietnamese studios were downloaded 9.6 billion times worldwide in 2024, and Google ranks Vietnam as the top mobile game exporter - ahead of every country, China included. [1]

Vietnam holds ~10.9% of global downloads in three casual genres. Vietnam accounts for 10.93 percent of all global downloads in Simulation, Puzzle and Arcade (about 3.3 billion installs), ahead of China (8.48%), Hong Kong (8.28%) and the US (7.20%) per Sensor Tower; those three genres are 66 percent of Vietnam-origin downloads. [1]

More than 3,000 studios industrialise hyper-casual production. Vietnam hosts more than 3,000 game studios. Standouts include Amanotes (world number-one music-game publisher by downloads 2018-2023, 3bn+ downloads), plus ABI Game Studio, OneSoft (1945 Air Force), Falcon and iKame - hyper-casual titles at industrial scale. [1]

The download-versus-revenue paradox: ARPDAU about $0.06. Vietnam's average revenue per daily active user excluding ads is about $0.06, roughly twelve times below a ~$0.70 benchmark. Colossal installs translate into little revenue per user - the defining economics of the market. [1]

Domestic top-grossing games are imported live-service titles. The titles that lead domestic revenue are imported live-service games, not homegrown ones - notably Garena's Lien Quan Mobile (Arena of Valor), effectively Vietnam's national game, and Free Fire. [1]

Decree 147 (25 Dec 2024) forced foreign game firms to localise or exit. Decree 147/2024 took effect on 25 December 2024. Foreign companies lost the option to serve Vietnam's 58.5 million gamers remotely: they must establish a Vietnamese legal entity with tax, real-name verification and data-residency compliance, or exit. It also bans casino-style games and adds a 16+ rating and playtime limits for under-18s. [2]

The state set a $1 billion games-revenue target for 2030. In November 2025 the Prime Minister's National Cultural Industries Strategy named software and entertainment games as one of 10 cultural industries, and the games regulator set a $1 billion revenue target for 2030 - framing games as an economic sector, not just a vice. [2]

G1 licensing shows high churn: 288 issued, 184 active (36% revoked). As of mid-November 2024, 288 G1 online-game licences had been issued with only 184 still active - a 36 percent revocation rate. Official processing windows are 20 days for G1 and 15 days for G2-G4, but only run from a valid application; entity formation and approvals come first. [2]

Riot kept Vietnamese esports via a VNGGames partnership. Riot Games continued its Vietnamese esports through a partnership with VNGGames for 2025 (announced August 2024), while Garena and VNG consolidated their positions by committing to Decree 147 compliance. The market rewired itself around licensed local entities in weeks. [2]

Sky Mavis built Axie Infinity; the Ronin hack stole about $620M. Vietnamese studio Sky Mavis developed Axie Infinity (2018), the play-to-earn blockchain game on its Ronin sidechain. In March 2022 hackers stole about US$620 million, linked to North Korea's Lazarus Group; the SLP token had already fallen over 99% from its peak. [3]

VNG is the domestic anchor with games, Zalo and payments. VNG (founded 2004) spans online games, Zalo, ZaloPay and Zing, with revenue of about VND 7.8 trillion in 2022. It became the official publisher of Roblox in Vietnam (2024) and has signalled an overseas listing ambition, though a large US IPO has not been completed. [4]

A deep gaming-outsourcing and co-development bench exists. Vietnam hosts established art-outsourcing and co-development studios - Sparx* (a Virtuos studio), Saigon Dragon Studios, Two Fingers and NUARE - which serve global publishers and form a skills base distinct from hyper-casual publishing. [5]

4. Forecast: Export Scale and the Revenue Gap

Through 2031: Vietnam stays the world's #1 mobile-game exporter. Prediction (high-ish confidence): Vietnam retains the top mobile-game export position by downloads through 2031, and its lead in Simulation, Puzzle and Arcade widens. Rationale: a 3,000-studio production system, low costs and demonstrated global reach compound. Falsified if another country overtakes Vietnam on Google/Sensor Tower export rankings before 2031. [1]

To 2028: export downloads keep growing, but revenue per user stays low. Prediction: Vietnam-origin downloads keep growing at double-digit rates, yet monetisation per user stays well below global averages. The export model shifts toward hybrid-casual (ads plus light in-app purchases) rather than closing the ARPU gap. Falsified if Vietnam's ARPDAU ex-ads approaches the $0.70 benchmark by 2028. [1]

To 2030: total revenue may near $1bn, but domestic mobile likely misses it. Prediction: on a total games-market basis (domestic plus captured export value) Vietnam can approach the stated $1 billion target by 2030, but domestic mobile revenue alone likely falls short; the target is better read as an industry-ecosystem goal than a domestic-consumer metric. Falsified if domestic mobile revenue alone exceeds $1 billion before 2030. [2]

5. Forecast: Regulation and Market Structure

To 2028: Decree 147 consolidates domestic publishing around local partners. Prediction: the licensing regime pushes domestic publishing toward a small set of licensed local entities (VNG/VNGGames, Garena and similar), while small foreign publishers exit or partner. This mirrors the Riot-VNGGames arrangement. Falsified if unlicensed foreign titles regain broad domestic distribution. [2]

To 2028: hyper-casual export studios are largely untouched by Decree 147. Prediction: because they publish to global app stores rather than serving Vietnamese players domestically, hyper-casual export studios remain mostly outside the licence regime and continue to drive export growth. Falsified if enforcement extends to export-oriented studios at scale. [1]

To 2027: licensing friction and revocations persist; enforcement is uneven. Prediction: the 36 percent G1 revocation rate signals ongoing attrition; processing will speed up on paper but stay unpredictable in practice, and enforcement will vary across platforms and provinces. Falsified if revocation rates fall sharply and timelines become consistently met. [2]

6. Forecast: Companies and Capital

To 2028: VNG stays the anchor; a large overseas IPO is unlikely before 2028. Prediction: VNG remains the domestic anchor across games, Zalo and payments, and a large overseas listing is unlikely before 2028 given market conditions and its US IPO not having completed. Falsified by a completed major international listing before 2028. [4]

To 2028: Sky Mavis pivots to Ronin infrastructure; no second Web3 mega-hit. Prediction: after the play-to-earn collapse and the ~$620 million Ronin hack, Sky Mavis leans toward Ronin infrastructure and supporting other studios, and Vietnam does not produce a second mass-market blockchain game hit by 2028. Falsified by a new Vietnamese Web3 title reaching mass scale. [3]

To 2028: M&A and consolidation among mid-size studios rise. Prediction: as hyper-casual margins compress (rising user-acquisition costs, AI-driven competition), mid-size Vietnamese studios consolidate or get acquired, while a top tier of exporters scales. Falsified if the studio count and independent mid-tier grow through 2028.

7. Forecast: Talent, Outsourcing and AI

To 2029: Vietnam grows as a co-development and AAA-outsourcing hub. Prediction: cost pressure at global publishers pushes more art, QA and co-development work to Vietnamese studios (Sparx*/Virtuos and peers), deepening a higher-skill segment alongside hyper-casual publishing. Falsified if outsourcing revenue and headcount stagnate. [5]

To 2028: AI tooling lifts output and compresses hyper-casual margins. Prediction: generative-AI tooling raises per-studio output and lowers the cost of casual content, intensifying competition and squeezing margins; AI-adopting studios pull ahead and laggards consolidate. Falsified if margins and studio counts are stable despite AI adoption.

To 2028: senior talent remains the binding constraint. Prediction: shortages of producers, technical leads and live-ops specialists - not capital or ideas - remain the main brake on scaling higher-value studios. Falsified by a clear surplus of senior talent or falling senior wages.

8. Forecast: Esports and Domestic Demand

To 2028: esports stays publisher-dependent; the state stays cautious. Prediction: Vietnamese esports remains tied to publisher programmes (e.g. Riot via VNGGames) and to a young, mobile-first audience, while the state keeps esports under cultural/regulatory caution rather than full-throated promotion. Falsified by state-led, publisher-independent esports leagues at scale. [2]

To 2031: mobile stays above 85% of players; young demand keeps growing. Prediction: mobile remains above 85 percent of the player base and domestic demand grows with a young, mobile-first population, even as revenue per user stays low. Falsified by a sharp shift to PC/console share or a demographic-driven decline. [1]

To 2030: policy increasingly frames games as a cultural export. Prediction: policy shifts further from treating games mainly as a vice toward promoting them as a cultural export industry, with targets, incentives and export promotion - unevenly implemented. Falsified by a return to restriction-first framing. [2]

9. Scenarios and Falsifiable Triggers

Base case: an export factory plus a licensed domestic market. Most likely path to 2031: Vietnam stays the world's mobile export factory; domestic publishing consolidates around licensed local players; revenue grows moderately; no AAA global breakout. The gap between downloads and revenue narrows only slightly.

Upside: a global hit plus policy support. Upside scenario (lower probability): a Vietnamese-made title breaks into global top-grossing, pairing with state export promotion, lifting domestic revenue well above $1.5 billion by 2031 and attracting investment and senior talent.

Downside: stricter enforcement plus an ad-market downturn. Downside scenario: tighter enforcement of Decree 147 combined with a global ad-market/UA downturn compresses export economics and consolidates the market faster than expected, with studio closures and talent emigration.

The four numbers that would settle the forecast. Watch: (1) Google/Sensor Tower export ranking, (2) ARPDAU ex-ads, (3) domestic revenue versus the $1bn/2030 target, and (4) G1 licence revocation rate. Movement in these four over 2027-2031 is the cleanest test of the predictions above.

10. Interpretation and confidence

The paper assesses an industry, not an individual or organisation, so the toolkit's 0-100 adverse-signal score has no subject. The probabilities attached to each forecast are calibrated judgements from indirect evidence, not statistical estimates, and market figures come from trackers that estimate gross rather than audited revenue.

11. Limitations

  • These are probabilistic forecasts, not certainties; each carries a confidence value and a falsifier so it can be scored later.
  • Market-size estimates disagree by method (about $817M total games in 2024; about $825M mobile in 2025; one tracker near $315M), so absolute levels are indicative, not precise.
  • Several baseline facts rest on trade-press and market trackers (Sensor Tower, GameGeek, PocketGamer) and encyclopaedic records; figures may be revised.
  • Regulatory details of Decree 147 and its enforcement are evolving; the analysis reflects sources available at the time of writing.
  • Company-level predictions (VNG, Sky Mavis) depend on private decisions and macro conditions that cannot be observed in advance.

12. Conclusion

The base case is continuity: export volume grows, per-user revenue stays low, publishing consolidates around licensed locals, and senior talent remains the binding constraint. The scenarios that would change the picture - an original-IP hit or genuine licensing liberalisation - are possible but not probable on present evidence.
