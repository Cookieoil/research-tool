# What It Would Take for Vietnam's Game Policy to Change - and How Likely

## Abstract

This paper asks what it would take for Vietnam's game policy to change - and how likely each change is. 'Change' means five things: licensing simplification and predictability; a rebalance from extraction to promotion; value-capture and talent upgrading; institutional capacity and consistency; and, at the limit, relaxing content/data controls. The preconditions largely exist on the promotion side: the 14th National Party Congress (19-23 Jan 2026) confirmed General Secretary To Lam and set 'breakthrough' priorities (private sector, digital transformation, institutional reform); a 2026 Digital Technology Law and investment-incentive rules (Decree 96) provide a promotion toolkit; economic pressure (thin ~$0.06 ARPU, AI-compressed margins) and the US 20% tariff push upgrading; and fiscal need supports better platform taxation. But the binding constraints do not move easily: institutional capacity was disrupted by the 2025 reorg (1,300+ procedures, ~630 unupdated by May 2025), and security/content agencies plus incumbent publishers resist deep liberalisation. The likely path is therefore 'more carrots, same fences': promotion-side incentives and modest licensing simplification are likely (55-75%), consolidation around licensed locals is likely (~75%), while meaningful content/data liberalisation (~30%), ending the local-entity requirement (~15%), independent esports (~25%) and a genuine value-capture upgrade (~35%) are unlikely before 2030-2031. The paper is interpretive and forward-looking.

## 1. Introduction

Reform feasibility is a question about coalitions and capacity, not about the merits of a policy. This paper states what would have to change for Vietnam's game policy to liberalise, identifies the actors who would have to move, and attaches calibrated probabilities to ten reform dimensions through 2031.

## 3. What Change Would Mean

**Licensing simplification and predictability.** Change would mean shorter, reliable G1 timelines, fewer capricious revocations, and clearer G2-G4 registration - reducing compliance friction without abandoning licensing.

**Rebalancing from extraction to promotion.** Change would shift the mix from localisation/tax extraction toward investment incentives, tax breaks and support for original IP and higher-value games.

**Value-capture and talent upgrading.** Change would mean deliberate support for design, original IP, co-development and senior talent - moving from assembly/downloads to higher-margin production.

**Institutional predictability and capacity.** Change would mean a capable, consistently enforcing civil service and stable rules across provinces - the governance precondition for investment in long-lived studios.

**Rebalancing control versus openness.** At the limit, change would mean relaxing content/data controls (real-name, data localisation) enough to broaden market access - the most sensitive dimension because it touches security and ideology.

## 4. What It Would Take

**Top-level political will — and the 2026 Congress supplied a mandate.** Change requires direction from the top. The 14th National Party Congress (19-23 Jan 2026) confirmed General Secretary To Lam and set 'breakthrough' priorities including the private sector, digital transformation and institutional reform - the political precondition for pro-industry reform. [1]

**A formal legal vehicle with incentives.** A 2026 Digital Technology Law providing legal foundations and strategic incentives, plus investment-incentive rules (Decree 96), give the state a promotion-side toolkit beyond licensing - the legal precondition. [2]

**Economic pressure to move up the value chain.** With ~$0.06 ARPDAU and AI compressing hyper-casual margins, the export-factory model is hitting diminishing returns - creating commercial pressure to support higher-value games and original IP. [3]

**Talent pressure and emigration risk.** If senior developers keep leaving for better pay abroad, the domestic ecosystem weakens; retention pressure can push the state and firms toward better conditions and incentives.

**External pressure from tariffs and rules of origin.** The US maintains a 20% reciprocal tariff and scrutinises transshipment and origin, pushing Vietnam toward higher domestic content and clearer rules - a reform accelerant. [4]

**FTA and digital-trade commitments.** CPTPP/EVFTA and digital-economy commitments create pressure to liberalise services, data and digital trade - a slow, treaty-driven reform channel. [5]

**Fiscal need to tax platforms properly.** The state's desire to capture platform revenue (Google ~$87M over 2018-2022) is a motive for better, not necessarily heavier, rules - reform can be fiscal-positive. [6]

**Institutional capacity is the binding constraint.** The 2025 reorg directly affected over 1,300 administrative procedures and left ~630 not yet updated by end-May 2025; until capacity and predictability improve, reform will be unevenly implemented. [7]

**Interest-group resistance is the main brake.** Security/content agencies and incumbent licensed publishers benefit from the status quo; meaningful liberalisation requires overcoming this coalition.

**A catalytic event could accelerate or reverse change.** A global Vietnamese hit, a fraud/gambling scandal, a tariff hike or an ad-market downturn could jolt the system - accelerating reform or triggering a crackdown depending on its nature.

## 5. How Likely Each Change Is

**LIKELY: promotion-side incentives keep expanding (≈75%).** Prediction: the promotion toolkit keeps growing (Digital Technology Law, Decree 96 incentives, cultural-industry support) through 2026-2028, because it is already legislated and serves the growth agenda. Falsified if the new incentives go unimplemented. [2]

**LIKELY: games kept as a priority cultural industry with tax breaks (≈70%).** Prediction: games remain a named priority industry with tax incentives for history/culture-themed titles through 2028, per the cultural-industries strategy. Falsified if games are dropped from the priority list or incentives are withdrawn. [8]

**LIKELY: continued consolidation around licensed locals (≈75%).** Prediction: Decree 147 consolidates domestic publishing around a small set of licensed locals through 2028, as the licensing regime bites. Falsified if unlicensed foreign titles regain broad distribution. [8]

**MEDIUM: moderate licensing simplification (≈55%).** Prediction: timelines and revocation practices improve modestly by 2028 (faster G1, fewer arbitrary revocations) without changing the regime's structure. Falsified if revocation rates stay high and delays persist.

**MEDIUM: talent retention improves via incentives (≈45%).** Prediction: incentives and career paths modestly slow senior emigration by 2029, but do not reverse it. Falsified if senior emigration accelerates.

**UNLIKELY: deeper content/data liberalisation (≈30%).** Prediction: real-name and data-localisation requirements stay substantially in place to 2030 because they serve security and fiscal goals. Falsified by a material relaxation of data-localisation or real-name rules.

**UNLIKELY: end of the local-entity publishing requirement (≈15%).** Prediction: the requirement that foreign publishers establish a Vietnamese entity persists to 2031; full liberalisation of foreign publishing is very unlikely. Falsified by its removal or a major carve-out.

**UNLIKELY: independent (publisher-independent) esports at scale (≈25%).** Prediction: esports stays publisher-dependent through 2030; state-led, publisher-independent leagues do not reach scale. Falsified by a major independent league.

**LOW-MEDIUM: a genuine value-capture upgrade (≈35%).** Prediction: a Vietnamese original-IP global hit remains more likely than a structural upgrade; a sustained move from assembly to high-value production by 2031 is possible but not probable. Falsified by a durable IP-led revenue shift.

**MEDIUM: implementation capacity improves enough to matter (≈45%).** Prediction: civil-service pay/capacity and cross-province consistency improve partially by 2029, but uneven enforcement persists. Falsified by a sharp, sustained improvement in procedures and predictability. [7]

## 6. Unblocking the Binding Constraints

**Civil-service capacity is the true prerequisite.** Because uneven capacity and 'fear of responsibility' slow approvals, the highest-leverage reform is apolitical: pay, training and performance systems that make officials willing and able to implement rules predictably.

**Deep liberalisation needs a shift in the security coalition.** Relaxing content/data controls requires the security and ideological agencies to accept less control - unlikely without a change in their influence, a major external commitment, or a crisis that reframes the trade-off.

**Leadership renewal at the 15th Congress (2031) is the next window.** The 14th Congress locked in continuity to 2031. A leadership transition at the 15th Congress is the next plausible moment for a step-change in the control-vs-growth balance.

## 7. Synthesis: The Likely Path

**Most likely path: promotion deepens, control persists.** The base case is incremental liberalisation on the incentive/promotion side while the licensing, localisation and content regime stays intact - 'more carrots, same fences'. This is the path of least resistance for a state that wants both growth and control.

**A two-track equilibrium is the stable outcome.** Export-oriented studios (outside the domestic licence regime) get room to grow, while domestic publishing stays tightly licensed - a de facto split that lets the state claim growth and control at once.

**The indicators that would confirm real change.** Watch: G1 processing time and revocation rate; implementation of Digital Technology Law incentives; the share of revenue from original IP; senior talent retention; and any relaxation of data-localisation or the local-entity requirement. Movement on these five would confirm genuine change.

## 9. Limitations

- Reform-feasibility analysis is interpretive and forward-looking; probabilities are subjective judgements, not statistical estimates.

## 10. Conclusion

The plausible path is more carrots and the same fences: promotion incentives expand, publishing consolidates around licensed locals and licensing is simplified at the margin, while deep content and data liberalisation and the end of the local-entity requirement remain unlikely before 2030-2031. Reform on the control side would require a coalition that does not currently exist.
