Abstract

Two existing reports are combined here. The first measured who plays male-marketed gacha: women are 40-45% of mixed-cast anime titles and 30-40% even in fanservice titles, and five mechanisms explain the crossover - a mislabelled category, an unguarded format, attachment-led monetisation, avatar identification, and a supply shortage. The second followed a female-oriented title, Reign of Nightfall, from a launch with 10 million pre-registrations to a monotonic decline. Read together, the reports separate two questions that the industry routinely conflates: whether an audience exists (it does) and whether a title can hold it (often not). Demand-side constraints govern entry; supply-side constraints - monetisation pressure, production quality, heroine fit and winner-take-all concentration - govern retention. The synthesis is an author's reading of two desk-research reports, not a new dataset: no probability sample of gacha players by gender exists, and the case study's sources are outlet-level rather than canonical URLs.

1. Scope and methodology

This paper is a synthesis of two prior desk-research reports, both produced on 21 November 2026. The first, 'Why So Many Women Play Male-Oriented Gacha Games', contributed 33 findings and 23 sources drawn from consumer surveys (ESA 2025, GEM Partners), market trackers (Sensor Tower, AppMagic, Gamma Data), peer-reviewed games research and trade press. Its finding blocks were carried over verbatim and only re-grouped for narrative order. The second, 'Reign of Nightfall Research', contributed the case study: launch economics, a rank-decay series, rating history, attributed causes and self-selected quit evidence for a 2026 Chinese female-oriented title. Its findings were transcribed from that report's text. Five cross-report findings were authored for this paper and are labelled as interpretation. No new fieldwork, scraping or survey was performed; no finding was re-typed from memory.

F1 — 48% of video-game players worldwide are women (ESA 2025)

  • Engine: survey_research · category: demand: measured audience · confidence: 0.85 (high)
  • Source: https://www.gamesindustry.biz/esa-48-of-video-game-players-are-women
  • Detail: The ESA's 2025 Global Power of Play, compiled with AudienceNet from 24,216 weekly players aged 16+ in 21 countries, reports 51% male and 48% female players globally. ESA president Stanley Pierre-Louis told GamesIndustry.biz that women outnumber men in 10 of the 21 countries, with two more at 50/50 (Brazil 57% women, South Africa 58%). Reading: the addressable audience of a mass-market game is near parity before genre effects are applied.

F2 — Mobile is the preferred device for 64% of women players

  • Engine: survey_research · category: demand: measured audience · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://www.gamesindustry.biz/esa-48-of-video-game-players-are-women
  • Detail: The same ESA report found mobile is the preferred device for 64% of women, versus about half of men, and for 55% of all players. Interpretation: a mobile-first, free-to-play, low-hardware-barrier genre meets the female audience where it already plays, unlike PC/console-native genres that require dedicated hardware and long sessions.

F3 — Japan: Genshin 45% female, Honkai: Star Rail 40%, against a 25% baseline

  • Engine: survey_research · category: demand: measured audience · confidence: 0.85 (high)
  • Source: https://kultur.jp/entertainment-average-ages-mtf-ratio
  • Detail: GEM Partners surveyed 180,000 Japanese respondents; Nikkei Entertainment's 'Oshikatsu MAP' aggregation (July 2025) reports male:female ratios of Genshin Impact 55:45 (average age 26), Honkai: Star Rail 60:40 (25), Zenless Zone Zero 75:25 (26), Blue Archive 90:10 (25), Touken Ranbu 2:98 (35) and Ensemble Stars 5:95 (25), against 75:25 (average age 30) for games overall. Reading: mixed-cast anime RPGs over-index women by roughly 1.6-1.8x the national baseline, explicitly female-oriented franchises are 95-98% female, and male-coded titles such as Blue Archive sit at 10%. Caveat: the survey measures series fans rather than active players, in one high-anime-literacy market.

F4 — The largest 'male-oriented' gacha are mixed-audience games

  • Engine: market_data · category: demand: measured audience · confidence: 0.70 (moderate)
  • Source: https://www.blog.udonis.co/mobile-marketing/mobile-games/genshin-impact-advertising
  • Detail: Genshin Impact is routinely called male-oriented because of its anime aesthetic and gacha/waifu lineage, yet its global player base is reported at roughly 60% male / 40% female (GamerBraves 2025, cited by Udonis), and the Japanese survey puts it at 45% female. Reading: 'male-oriented' is a marketing and genre label inherited from anime/otaku media, not a measured description of who plays. That label is doing much of the work in the premise of this paper.

F5 — NIKKE is 40% female in Korea and 30% in Japan

  • Engine: market_data · category: demand: measured audience · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://www.famitsu.com/news/202312/13327628.html
  • Detail: Famitsu, citing Sensor Tower's Store Intelligence, reported that NIKKE earned over $600m worldwide between 4 November 2022 and 10 November 2023, with Japan about 60% of revenue, the US about 16% and Korea about 12%. Its user split was 70:30 male:female in Japan and 60:40 in Korea - the highest female share of its three main markets. NIKKE is an extreme case, marketed on sexualised female designs, so 30-40% female shows that even the most male-coded gacha is not a male-only space.

F6 — The category contains three different audiences, not one

  • Engine: press_reporting · category: demand: measured audience · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://kultur.jp/entertainment-average-ages-mtf-ratio
  • Detail: The same Japanese survey separates (a) explicitly female-oriented franchises at 95-98% female (Touken Ranbu, Ensemble Stars), (b) mixed-cast anime RPGs at 40-45% female (Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail), and (c) male-coded titles at 1-25% female (Blue Archive 10%, Zenless Zone Zero 25%, Pro Baseball Spirits 5%, eFootball 1%). Synthesis: most of the surprise in 'why so many women play male-oriented gacha' disappears once case (b) is recognised as a mixed-audience category and case (a) as a separate, near-single-gender market.

F7 — Voice-chat competitive games are measurably hostile to women

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.75 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444812458271
  • Detail: Kuznekoff and Rose (2012) observed 245 multiplayer Halo 3 matches against 1,660 unique players, broadcasting either a male or a female voice; the female voice received three times as many negative comments as the male voice or no voice. Gacha titles are predominantly single-player PvE with optional co-op and no voice chat (Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail and Love and Deepspace are all single-player or co-op-optional). Interpretation: the exclusion mechanism documented in competitive 'male' genres is structurally absent, so the demographic gate is weaker.

F8 — Sexism in online games is modelled through masculine norms, not content

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.70 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2013.07.014
  • Detail: Fox and Tang (2014) examine sexism in online games as a function of conformity to masculine norms and social dominance orientation, rather than as a function of a game's content or genre. Reading: this supports treating 'male-oriented' as a property of the social environment - who is present and how they behave - rather than of the art style alone. Combined with Kuznekoff and Rose's voice experiment, it explains how a game can have sexy female characters without reproducing the hostility that pushes women out.

F9 — Player motivation is multidimensional, not a gender binary

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.75 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.1089/cpb.2006.9.772
  • Detail: Yee's (2006) factor-analytic model identifies ten motivation subcomponents grouped into three overarching components - achievement, social and immersion - and relates them to age, gender and usage patterns. Reading: the standard instrument for 'why people play' does not reduce to 'men like competition, women like stories'; it treats motivation as a profile across dimensions. Genre-level gender gaps are therefore better explained by access, social environment and content than by a single preference axis.

F10 — Honkai: Star Rail's Phainon banner earned ~JPY 1.8bn on Japanese iOS

  • Engine: market_data · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://www.pockettactics.com/honkai-star-rail/phainon-banner-sales
  • Detail: alone Pocket Tactics (Holly Alice, 7 July 2025), citing Sensor Tower data, reports that the Phainon banner took Honkai: Star Rail to #1 on the Chinese charts and #1 on Japanese iOS, with an estimated $12.5m (JPY 1.8bn) from Japanese iOS users alone. The article frames this against the industry belief that 'male characters don't sell' and speaks directly to 'husbando collectors'; for comparison it cites Acheron (~$50m) and Ruan Mei (~$35m) as the game's biggest banners.

F11 — Male characters are designed and voiced for female attachment

  • Engine: press_reporting · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.70 (moderate)
  • Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaedehara_Kazuha
  • Detail: HK01's Lin Zhuoheng (13 June 2023) called Kaedehara Kazuha one of Genshin Impact's most popular male characters, credited his design (silver hair, ronin, Japanese sword, warm personality) and wrote that Nobunaga Shimazaki's voice acting 'particularly captivated many female players'. The same record documents fan art, roleplay and cosplay around the character. Reading: the husbando channel is not incidental - design, voice casting and narrative arc are the product.

F12 — Parasocial intimacy is the monetisation mechanism, and it is studied

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.65 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.17645/mac.8662
  • Detail: A 2025 qualitative study of Chinese female otome players finds that parasocial romantic relationships are embedded in daily life through game mechanics and imagination, with players 'focusing on the emotional support provided by male characters'; the authors link this to emotional regulation, self-identity and redefined expectations of real relationships (51 citations). The same mechanism operates in mixed-cast gacha: characters are sold as relationships through voice lines, birthday events, messages and skins, and the collection loop converts attachment into spending. Interpretation: the character's gender matters less than the relationship the game scripts.

F13 — 'Husbando' is the exact symmetric counterpart of 'waifu'

  • Engine: press_reporting · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waifu
  • Detail: Wikipedia defines waifu as a fictional female character toward whom one feels romantic or sexual attraction, and husbando as the male equivalent; both are described as forms of parasocial relationship. Reading: the affective engine of the genre is not intrinsically male. It was marketed to men first, but the same grammar - attractive character, voice, story, collection - transfers directly to a female audience.

F14 — Women cross-gender about a third as often as men

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://www.concordia.ca/cunews/main/releases/2014/05/06/video-game-avataar.html
  • Detail: Three datasets triangulate the avatar question. In a peer-reviewed World of Warcraft study (N=375, recorded behaviour during a custom quest), 23% of male participants chose an opposite-gender avatar and 7% of female participants did (2014). In Quantic Foundry's 2021 survey, 29% of men said they prefer playing female characters and 9% of women said they prefer male characters. Riot's internal League of Legends data is an outlier in the same direction: 'something like 97% of female players only play female champions', with men split 50/50 (F15). Reading: roughly one in ten women crosses gender, so women are not exclusive - but they are about three times more gender-loyal than men. A female-majority roster is therefore not a barrier, and the modal female preference for a female avatar explains why female-led marketing can work with women. Caveat: these measure avatars (the self), not collectibles (the other); the husbando evidence is a separate axis.

F15 — Riot's 97% is a League-specific outlier

  • Engine: market_data · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.60 (moderate)
  • Source: https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/17395/riot-97-of-female-league-of-legends-players-only-play-female-champions
  • Detail: Riot lead gameplay producer Ryan 'Reav3' Mireles, responding to a player complaint in June 2022, said: 'Our data shows that female players primarily play female champions, in fact, it's something like 97% of female players only play female champions. Male players are evenly split... If anything, whenever we make a male champion, we are abandoning most of our female players.' Caveat: internal, unpublished, hedged ('something like'), and measured as 'only play' in a game where most players main a small champion pool. It is an outlier against the WoW (23%/7%) and Quantic (29%/9%) figures. Reading: directional evidence that League's female players strongly prefer female champions, not a general law about women in games.

F16 — Games systematically over-represent men

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.85 (high)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.1177/1461444809105354
  • Detail: A sales-weighted content analysis of 150 games across nine platforms (Williams, Martins, Consalvo and Ivory, 2009; 798 citations) found 'a systematic over-representation of males, white and adults and a systematic under-representation of females, Hispanics, Native Americans, children and the elderly' relative to the US population. Reading: a male avatar is the unmarked default of the medium and a female one is the marked, scarcer option. Scarcity is why the same behaviour - choosing one's own gender - carries more weight for women: it is a representation claim, not only an aesthetic preference.

F17 — Female protagonists matter more to women than to men

  • Engine: survey_research · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.75 (moderate)
  • Source: https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/for-most-women-and-girls-female-protagonists-matter-says-survey
  • Detail: Quantic Foundry asked 1,266 gamers to rate 'having the option to play a female protagonist' on a five-point scale. 75% of female players rated it very or extremely important, against about 33% of male players; the share choosing 'extremely important' was roughly triple among women (reported by Game Developer, 30 August 2017). Reading: this is asymmetry in importance, not just in behaviour - women are not merely picking female avatars more often, they report the option as mattering more. Caveat: self-selected online survey from 2017, and the question asks about the availability of the option rather than forcing a choice.

F18 — Content analysis: female leads are common, but often sexualised

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.1007/s11199-006-9158-0
  • Detail: Jansz and Martis (2007) analysed the introductory films of 12 contemporary games and found that female characters appeared in leading parts as often as male characters, but were portrayed with a sexualised emphasis on female features; most characters were White, and heroes exclusively so. Reading: the 'Lara phenomenon' shows that competent female protagonists are established in games - it is content evidence, not evidence about women's preferences. The preference evidence in this paper comes from the avatar studies above (F14-F17).

F19 — Sexualised avatars can increase women's self-objectification

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.75 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.1177/0361684314553578
  • Detail: Two experiments (N=87 and N=81) found that college women exposed to sexualised avatars reported higher self-objectification than those exposed to non-sexualised avatars; in the second study self-objectification mediated a subsequent increase in rape-myth acceptance (2014). Caveat: this does not mean sexualised designs repel players - NIKKE's 40% female share in Korea and women's preference for female champions show otherwise - but it does mean 'women like it' cannot settle the design question.

F20 — Fan labour grows the market

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.70 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.17011/ht/urn.201911265024
  • Detail: A 2019 study of otome game communities ('Growing the Otome Game Market: Fan Labor and Otome Game Communities Online', 54 citations) documents how fans produce art, fiction, translations and discussion that lower acquisition costs and sustain engagement. Reading: female-heavy fandoms are an asset for live-service games, which is one reason mixed-cast titles cultivate them.

F21 — Fan art, roleplay and cosplay are documented retention channels

  • Engine: press_reporting · category: demand: mechanisms · confidence: 0.60 (moderate)
  • Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaedehara_Kazuha
  • Detail: The Kazuha reception record documents players expressing appreciation through fan art, roleplay and cosplay, and NIKKE's Western cosplay scene is cited by community members as a reason the game reaches women despite its marketing. Caveat: the NIKKE claim is anecdotal; the Kazuha record is press-sourced. Interpretation: visible fandom acts as social proof that lowers the perceived cost of entering a 'male' space.

F22 — China's female-oriented games market reached RMB 8bn, growing 124% in

  • Engine: market_data · category: supply: market structure · confidence: 0.85 (high)
  • Source: https://kr-asia.com/women-power-chinas-usd-1-billion-romance-gaming-market
  • Detail: 2024 Nikkei Asia, republished by KrASIA (5 August 2025), reports that female-oriented games grew 124% year on year in 2024 to RMB 8bn (about $1.1bn), driven by Love and Deepspace, according to Gamma Data. Tencent's Light and Night and NetEase's Beyond the World are cited as competitors expanding into the segment. Both incumbents are 2D romance titles, which makes Love and Deepspace's 3D presentation a visible step change. Reading: demand was latent, not absent; capital followed proof.

F23 — High-production female-oriented games were rare

  • Engine: press_reporting · category: supply: market structure · confidence: 0.60 (moderate)
  • Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_and_Deepspace
  • Detail: Love and Deepspace is a 3D otome built as a live-service action RPG with gacha 'wishes', fully voiced cutscenes and real-time companion interactions - production values historically associated with male-oriented anime RPGs, not otome visual novels. Interpretation: many women were not choosing 'male-oriented content' so much as the highest-production anime games available; when comparable production arrived in a female-oriented title, they moved and paid.

F24 — Love and Deepspace's revenue quantifies the latent demand

  • Engine: market_data · category: supply: market structure · confidence: 0.80 (high)
  • Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_and_Deepspace
  • Detail: Wikipedia's commercial-performance summary, drawing on Sensor Tower and market-research reports, records over $500m in global revenue by March 2025 (40% of it outside China) and about $360m in 2025, when it ranked second among anime-style mobile games. Reading: a female-oriented title can reach the top tier of the anime-gacha market, so this audience is not marginal.

F25 — Female players are high-value spenders

  • Engine: market_data · category: supply: market structure · confidence: 0.75 (moderate)
  • Source: https://kr-asia.com/women-power-chinas-usd-1-billion-romance-gaming-market
  • Detail: Nikkei's reporting includes a 22-year-old who had spent about RMB 6,000 ($840) on in-game cards; 1,200 women won places at a Bilibili World photo event with the game's male leads; and Papergames' Tmall store ranked second in trendy toys behind Pop Mart. 86Research analyst Charlie Chai told Nikkei that Chinese female consumers are 'quite monetization-friendly' and 'a gold mine' in revenue terms.

F26 — Male banners can top the charts, so publishing incentives are shifting

  • Engine: market_data · category: supply: market structure · confidence: 0.70 (moderate)
  • Source: https://www.pockettactics.com/honkai-star-rail/phainon-banner-sales
  • Detail: Phainon's banner reached #1 in China and Japan, and Pocket Tactics argues publishers should 'realise that players do want cool boys on their teams'. HoYoverse's mixed roster - Kazuha, Zhongli, Neuvillette, Phainon - keeps serving the female segment, while Tencent and NetEase are funding female-oriented titles. Reading: the commercial case for treating male-oriented games as male-only is weakening.

F27 — Publisher behaviour, not player preference, is the moving part

  • Engine: press_reporting · category: supply: market structure · confidence: 0.70 (moderate)
  • Source: https://kr-asia.com/women-power-chinas-usd-1-billion-romance-gaming-market
  • Detail: The Nikkei/KrASIA piece describes developers 'doubling down' on the RMB 8bn female-oriented industry after Love and Deepspace, and Bilibili's spokesperson notes that young women are also active in strategy and competitive genres 'traditionally considered male-dominated'. Synthesis: category labels follow investment, and investment is following demonstrated female spending.

F33 — Cause 3: a fixed heroine breaks the projection the genre assumes

F42 — Spending harms are documented - and skew female in one sample

  • Engine: peer_reviewed · category: critical considerations · confidence: 0.75 (moderate)
  • Source: https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyt.2022.940281
  • Detail: A 2022 cross-sectional survey of 337 gacha players aged 18-25 in Hong Kong found about 40% at moderate problem-gambling risk and 25% at high risk; women were 78.6% of the high-risk group versus 39.7% of the non/low-risk group, and stress, anxiety and monthly gacha spending explained 51.3% of the variance in problem-gambling scores. Caveat: single city, cross-sectional, self-report - but it cuts against any account that treats female participation as purely benign.

5. Limitations and confidence

  • This is a synthesis, not a new study. It inherits every limitation of its two source reports and adds no primary data.
  • No probability sample of gacha players by gender exists in the public domain. Headline percentages are consumer surveys, publisher/platform estimates or community counts, and several are self-selected.
  • The demand-side finding blocks were extracted programmatically from the published PDF and re-grouped; wording is preserved but section order differs from the original paper.
  • The case study's citations are outlet-level (GamerScroll, TapTap, 17173, 36Kr, Qimai, Adquan, Huxiu). Canonical article URLs were not preserved in the source report, so only the official site and the GamerScroll domain appear in the source register.
  • Revenue and rank figures for the case study are third-party estimates and rank is a proxy for revenue, not revenue itself.
  • The five cross-report findings are the author's interpretation. Their confidence values are judgements, not computed intervals.
  • The demand-side report's own caveats stand: store-model estimates are unaudited, the GEM survey measures series fans in one high-anime-literacy market, and company internal data (Riot's 97%) is an unpublished outlier.
  • Absence of a finding is not proof of absence; sources that were skipped or blocked are noted in the findings.
  • Public data changes. Re-verify sources before relying on a paper that is no longer current.
  • Non-English source material is translated into English; original wording is kept only as a clearly marked secondary reference.
