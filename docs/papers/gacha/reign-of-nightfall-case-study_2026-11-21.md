# High Pre-Registration, Monotonic Decay: A Commercial and Design Post-Mortem of Reign of Nightfall (2026)

## Abstract

*Reign of Nightfall* (夜幕之下, "Under the Night Curtain") is a female-oriented Chinese mobile RPG that entered with more than ten million pre-registrations in June 2026 and then declined monotonically. This paper reconstructs its commercial trajectory and evaluates the causes that have been attributed to it. The evidence is consistent with a launch-quality and monetisation-timing failure rather than a demand failure: the title recruited an audience and then lost it. Monthly average grossing rank fell from 78.3 in June to 125 in July, 139 in August and 153 by November, and the title left the top 200 before the end of the review window; successive version peaks decayed from 25 to 37, 65 and 71, meaning each content release recovered less than the last. Four causes are evaluated: monetisation pressure before attachment formation, combat and content quality below the category bar, a fixed heroine that displaces the player's projection, and concentration in a winner-take-all segment where one competitor took roughly 40 percent of revenue. Attribution strength differs by cause, and the paper states which claims rest on explicit evidence and which on inference. It closes with a falsifiable decision rule for grading the title's next flagship update.

**Keywords:** case study, female-oriented games, live-service decay, monetisation, player attrition, China.

## 1. The question

A title with ten million pre-registrations is not short of demand. When it declines anyway, the interesting question is not "why did nobody play it" but "why did the players who arrived leave". This paper treats *Reign of Nightfall* as a retention case and asks which of the attributed causes the evidence actually supports.

Two framing points matter. Rank is a relative measure: a growing market can sink a title's rank even while its absolute revenue holds, and the estimates used here are third-party gross figures rather than audited accounts. And a case study establishes mechanisms, not base rates — one title shows how failure happens, not how often.

## 4. Market context

The market the title entered is large, fast-growing and concentrated. China's female-oriented segment reached RMB 8.0 billion in 2024, up 124.1 percent year on year [7, 8]. In 2025, 24 female-oriented titles entered the iOS top-600 grossing chart and earned approximately RMB 2.731 billion combined — and the single leader, *Love and Deepspace*, took nearly half [7, 8]. The structure is winner-take-all with a thin mid-tier.

Three consequences follow for a new entrant. Production values are the entry ticket, because the incumbent sets the bar. Attachment is the asset being competed for, because the leader's advantage is an installed emotional base. And the mid-tier has little room: a title that misses the top of the chart competes for a small residual.

## 6. Attributing the causes

Four causes have been advanced. They differ in how well the evidence supports them.

**Cause 1 — monetisation pressure at launch (attribution: strong).** Hard pity at 80 pulls, soft pity at 60, an expected cost of roughly RMB 1,300 per limited character, a two-week banner cadence and fewer than 20 free pulls per month [5]. The model asks for spending before the player has formed an attachment, which inverts the attachment-led monetisation that works elsewhere in the category. The pity mathematics and banner cadence are verifiable from the game's own systems, which makes this the best-supported explanation.

```chart-stats

| 60 | Soft pity (pulls) |
| ~RMB 1,300 | Expected cost per limited character |
| <20 | Free pulls per month |
caption: Figure 5. The model asks for payment before attachment forms - the inverse of the attachment-led monetisation that works elsewhere in the category [5].
```

**Cause 2 — content quality below the category bar (attribution: moderate).** Reviewers rated the combat "cheap, low-quality and visually noisy" at about 5.4 out of 10, against 8 or higher for story and art. The title competed on production values in a category where production values are the entry ticket. The evidence is reviewer consensus rather than user behaviour, so it is inferred rather than demonstrated [4, 5].

**Cause 3 — a fixed heroine (attribution: moderate, interpretive).** The protagonist was written with a strong fixed personality, which commentators argued breaks the self-insertion the genre depends on: in a female-oriented game the heroine is the player's avatar, so a strongly characterised heroine is a design conflict rather than a characterisation choice. This is a design argument supported by commentary rather than by measurement.

**Cause 4 — winner-take-all concentration (attribution: strong, structural).** With the segment leader taking roughly 40 percent of revenue, a new entrant must out-produce an incumbent with a far larger budget and an established attachment base. This is a structural constraint that does not depend on the title's own execution.

**The quit evidence (attribution: weak, by design).** Verbatim quitting posts on TapTap and Tieba cite monetisation, content quality and the heroine [3, 9]. They are illustrative and self-selected: they document individual attrition and articulate sentiment, but they cannot establish a churn rate.
## 7. Outlook and a falsifiable decision rule

The decayed peak series implies structural rather than cyclical decline. If the next flagship banner fails to set a new high, the base case is a long-tail or maintenance phase; for a low-cost 2D title, the more typical outcome than a hard shutdown is a stop-content, keep-servers wind-down.

A decision rule makes the judgement gradeable rather than rhetorical:

| Next flagship peak | Reading | Expected management response |
|---|---|---|
| ~40th or better | Stabilising into a durable mid-tier long tail | Cadence maintained |
| ~60th to 100th | Soft decline; erosion continues | Cadence cuts, heavier paid-only monetisation |
| Worse than ~120th | Structural decline confirmed | Cadence cuts and aggressive monetisation; elevated wind-down probability within 12–18 months |

Table 4. Falsifiable decision rule for the next flagship update [2].

The subjective probability attached to a weak flagship peak is about 65–75 percent, and about 70 percent for the June 2027 anniversary. These are calibrated judgements from indirect evidence — rank rather than revenue — not measured probabilities.

## 8. Method and sources

The study was conducted with open-source tooling: web search, regional (Chinese-language) search and direct page retrieval, with non-English material translated into English for the report. Principal sources were the game's store listing and rank series, platform forum threads (launch ranks, first-month figures, quit and uninstall threads), Chinese games media (17173, 36Kr, Adquan, Huxiu), market trackers (Qimai via aggregators) and the publisher's own site and wiki records for identity and version history [6].

**Confidence note.** Identity and timeline facts are high-confidence. Revenue and rank figures are third-party estimates and are gross, not net. Attribution of churn is strongest for monetisation and gating, where explicit quit posts combine with pity mathematics and rank collapse, and weaker for combat art, story and heroine design, where dissatisfaction is inferred from rating collapse rather than from explicit statements.

## 9. Caveats

- Rank is a relative proxy, not revenue; a growing market can sink a title's rank while absolute revenue holds.
- All economic figures are third-party estimates; the publisher publishes no audited financials.
- Quotes are self-selected public posts: evidence of sentiment and of specific individual attrition, not a measured churn rate.
- Version peaks are single-day ranks, and seasonal competition distorts comparison.
- Probability figures are the author's judgement, not data-derived.
- Some Chinese-language sources blocked direct retrieval and were quoted from search snippets that match fetchable sources.

## 10. Conclusion

*Reign of Nightfall* is a retention case wearing the costume of a demand case. Its failure was not that an audience did not exist — ten million pre-registrations and a first-month gross above US$3.31 million show otherwise — but that the title's monetisation front-loaded pressure before attachment, its production fell below the category bar, and its design displaced the player's projection in a genre built on it, inside a segment where one competitor holds roughly 40 percent of revenue.

The practical lesson for developers is that pre-registration volume measures reach, not retention, and that the first fortnight is where the attachment contract is either established or broken. The methodological lesson is the decision rule in Table 4: state in advance what outcome would count as stabilisation, and grade the next update against it.
