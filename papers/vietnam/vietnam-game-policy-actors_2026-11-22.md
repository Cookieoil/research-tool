# Who Decides? The Institutions and People Behind Vietnam's Game Policy

## Abstract

This paper identifies who actually makes Vietnam's game-policy decisions. The answer is a chain rather than a single person. At the top, the Political Bureau - led by General Secretary To Lam - issued Resolution 57-NQ/TW on 22 December 2024, setting the science, technology and digital-transformation line that frames games as a digital industry. The National Assembly then authorised the 2025 restructuring by Resolution 176/2025/QH15 (18 February 2025), and the Government (headed by Prime Minister Pham Minh Chinh) issued Decree 147/2024/ND-CP on 9 November 2024 (effective 25 December). The text was drafted and is administered by the Ministry of Information and Communications through its Authority of Broadcasting and Electronic Information (ABEI). In the 2025 overhaul, MIC was split: parts went to the Ministry of Science and Technology (led by Nguyen Manh Hung, the former MIC minister, as the 'core force' for Resolution 57) and parts - including ABEI and the game file - to the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, whose ABEI Director General Le Quang Tu Do is the operational face of game policy. The Ministry of Public Security (Luong Tam Quang) and the Ministry of Finance co-own enforcement and tax. Domestic publishers (VNG, Garena/VNGGames), studios and global platforms (Google, Meta, ByteDance, Roblox, Riot) influence at the margins through consultation and GameVerse, but the Party-state sets the rules. The paper is interpretive, drawn from legal, government and industry sources.

## 1. Introduction

Game policy is made by a chain, not a committee. This paper maps the institutions and office-holders behind Vietnam's online-game regime, from Politburo direction through the National Assembly and the Government to the licensing agency and the enforcement bodies, and states where industry influence actually operates.

## 3. Party-Level Direction

**Politburo Resolution 57 (22 Dec 2024) sets the digital agenda.** Resolution 57-NQ/TW of the Political Bureau, dated 22 December 2024, sets out 'breakthroughs in the development of science, technology, innovation and national digital transformation'. It is the top-level Party directive that frames the digital push, including games as a digital industry. [1]

**MOST is designated the 'core force' to implement Resolution 57.** At the merger ceremony, the Science and Technology minister said the new ministry's mission is to realise Resolution 57 in science, technology, innovation and digital transformation - making MOST the implementing engine of the Party's digital line. [2]

**The Politburo and General Secretary set direction; the state executes.** The Political Bureau - led by the General Secretary (To Lam) - issues the directing resolutions, while government ministries turn them into law and practice. Party direction precedes and steers the regulatory instruments. [1]

## 4. The Decree-Maker

**Decree 147 was issued by the Government on 9 November 2024.** Decree 147/2024/ND-CP on managing and providing internet services and online information was issued by the Government on 9 November 2024 and took effect on 25 December 2024, replacing Decree 72/2013 and its amendments. [3]

**Government decrees are framed by the Prime Minister's cabinet.** Decrees are Government instruments; the Prime Minister, Pham Minh Chinh, heads the Government that issues them and chairs the national digital-transformation machinery. The PM's office therefore co-owns the legal vehicle even when a ministry drafts the text. [3]

**The National Assembly authorises the reform frame by resolution.** The early-2025 restructuring was approved by Resolution No 176 of the National Assembly dated 18 February 2025. The legislature sets the legal frame within which the Government and ministries then operate. [4]

## 5. The Lead Ministry and Drafting Agency

**MIC, through ABEI, drafted and administered Decree 147.** The Ministry of Information and Communications (MIC), via its Authority of Broadcasting and Electronic Information (ABEI), was the agency that drafted and administered Decree 147 and the online-game licensing regime. ABEI is the specialised regulator for broadcasting and electronic information. [3]

**ABEI is the operational regulator for online games.** The official game portal describes ABEI as the governing authority for online video games, defining the G1-G4 classes, licensing and registration conditions, and operator obligations. [5]

## 6. The 2024-2025 Restructuring

**Resolution 176 (18 Feb 2025) split MIC's functions between two ministries.** The 2025 restructuring divided the responsibilities of the Ministry of Information and Communications, transferring parts to the Ministry of Science and Technology and parts to the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism (guided by Decrees 43/2025 and 55/2025). [4]

**MOST absorbed MIC; Nguyen Manh Hung leads the merged ministry.** On 6 March 2025 MOST announced its new structure after merging with MIC (Decree 55/2025), with Minister Nguyen Manh Hung - the former MIC minister - at the helm of the merged science-and-technology ministry. [2]

**The games regulator now sits under the culture ministry.** After the split, the Authority of Broadcasting and Electronic Information - and thus the online-game file - sits under the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, moving games from the tech/information ministry to the culture ministry. [6]

**The fast-track reform affected over 1,300 administrative procedures.** The restructuring transferred licensing authority and procedures between ministries, directly affecting more than 1,300 administrative procedures; by end-May 2025 about 630 had not yet been updated - a disruption that touches game licensing continuity. [4]

## 7. The Named Officials

**To Lam: General Secretary who drives the streamlining and digital agenda.** As General Secretary (from August 2024), To Lam leads the Political Bureau that issued Resolution 57 and has driven the 'streamlining the apparatus' reform that reorganised ministries in 2025 - the political centre of gravity above game policy. [1]

**Pham Minh Chinh: Prime Minister heading the decree-issuing Government.** Prime Minister Pham Minh Chinh heads the Government that issued Decree 147 and chairs the national digital-transformation machinery, making his office a co-owner of the legal vehicle and the digital-economy push. [3]

**Nguyen Manh Hung: Science and Technology minister and Resolution 57 lead.** Nguyen Manh Hung, the former MIC minister, now heads the merged Ministry of Science and Technology and publicly frames MOST as the 'core force' to realise Resolution 57 - a key architect of the tech-side agenda. [2]

**Le Quang Tu Do: the operational face of game policy.** Le Quang Tu Do, Director General of ABEI under the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism, is the official who fronted Vietnam GameVerse 2026 and publicly sets out the pro-industry line (games as a priority cultural industry, tax incentives). He is the operational centre of the game file. [6]

**Nguyen Van Hung: Culture, Sports and Tourism minister.** The Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism - now the home of ABEI and the game file - is led by its minister (Nguyen Van Hung, in office since 2021); the culture ministry is therefore the cabinet-level owner of game policy after the 2025 reorg.

**Luong Tam Quang: Public Security minister overseeing cyber enforcement.** The Ministry of Public Security - led by Luong Tam Quang (minister from 2024) - gained expanded state-management functions in the 2025 reorg and underpins cyber/data enforcement that shapes online-game compliance.

## 8. Co-Deciding and Enforcement Ministries

**The Ministry of Public Security expanded its remit over information.** The 2025 restructuring broadened the Ministry of Public Security's authority, taking over state-management functions from several ministries, including MIC. Security agencies are therefore co-deciders on content and data rules. [4]

**The Ministry of Finance (absorbed MPI) owns the tax lever.** The Ministry of Planning and Investment was merged into the Ministry of Finance in 2025. Finance owns the cross-border-platform tax machinery that underpins the localisation/taxation motive behind Decree 147. [4]

## 9. Industry and Platform Actors

**Licensed domestic publishers are co-implementers of the regime.** VNG and Garena/VNGGames are the licensed domestic publishers that operate the top live-service games and implement the licensing rules. They are co-implementers of the regime rather than its authors. [6]

**A large exporter base: 210 active studios and 27,388 games in 2025.** Industry figures cited at GameVerse 2026: about 210 actively operating studios, 27,388 new games launched in 2025 (+13%), around 75 new games a day, and Funtap CEO Dao Quang Tuan among the visible industry voices. [6]

**Global platforms co-shape the market and the conversation.** Google (with a senior Play ecosystem executive), Meta, ByteDance, Roblox and Riot Games attend GameVerse, and Google Play data anchors the industry narrative - so foreign platforms are powerful stakeholders even though they do not write the rules. [6]

**GameVerse is the state-industry convening platform.** Vietnam GameVerse (since 2023), opened in 2026 by ABEI's Director General as head of the organising board, is where regulators, domestic developers, foreign platforms and esports meet - the main structured channel of industry influence. [7]

## 10. Synthesis: The Decision Chain

**The decision chain runs Party -> Assembly -> Government -> ministry -> enforcement.** The chain is: the Political Bureau (Resolution 57) sets direction; the National Assembly authorises the frame (Resolution 176/2025); the Government issues the decree (147/2024); a lead ministry drafts and administers (MIC/ABEI, then MST plus MCST/ABEI); and security, finance and police agencies enforce. No single actor decides alone.

**The operational centre of gravity is ABEI under MCST.** After the 2025 reorg, the practical centre of game policy is ABEI under the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism (Le Quang Tu Do), operating under the Political Bureau's digital line and a Government framework - with MOST holding the broader tech/digital mandate.

**Industry and platforms influence at the margins; the state decides.** Publishers, studios and global platforms shape implementation and the public narrative (consultations, GameVerse, data), but the licensing, localisation and content rules are set by the Party-state. Influence is consultative, not decisive.

## 13. Conclusion

Authority is concentrated at the top and administration at the agency. Because the chain is short, one reorganisation - such as the 2025 split of the information ministry - moves the entire regime, and because industry sits outside the chain its influence is consultative rather than decisive. That structure, not any individual, explains the policy's stability.
